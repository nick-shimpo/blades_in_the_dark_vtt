# Supporting rules the sheet has to know

Rules from the core rulebook that an interactive sheet must implement, display, or offer as an action. Grouped by the region of the sheet they affect. Page numbers are printed pages.

---

## 1. Actions: one-line descriptions for tooltips (pp. 58 to 59)

| Action | When you... |
|---|---|
| Attune | open your mind to the ghost field or other arcane power. |
| Command | compel swift obedience. |
| Consort | socialize with friends and contacts. |
| Finesse | employ dextrous manipulation or subtle misdirection. |
| Hunt | carefully track a target. |
| Prowl | traverse skillfully and quietly. |
| Skirmish | entangle a target in close combat so they can't easily escape. |
| Study | scrutinize details and interpret evidence. |
| Survey | observe the situation and anticipate outcomes. |
| Sway | influence with guile, charm, or argument. |
| Tinker | fiddle with devices and mechanisms. |
| Wreck | unleash savage force. |

Zero-dot actions roll 2d and keep the lowest; pushing yourself always gives at least 1d (p. 13, p. 51). Full descriptions with examples are on pp. 170 to 181.

## 2. The action roll (pp. 18 to 23)

- Pool: one d6 per action dot, plus bonus dice. Result is the highest die: 1 to 3 bad outcome, 4/5 partial success with consequence, 6 full success, two or more 6s critical.
- Position: controlled, risky (default), desperate. Effect: limited, standard, great. A **desperate** roll marks 1 xp in the action's attribute (p. 48).
- Bonus dice: push yourself (2 stress) for +1d, or accept a Devil's Bargain for +1d (not both), plus one assist (+1d).
- Pushing also buys +1 effect or acting while incapacitated (2 stress each, each once per action).

## 3. Stress, trauma and death (pp. 13 to 14, p. 33)

- Trauma conditions and their sheet descriptions:
  - **Cold:** You're not moved by emotional appeals or social bonds.
  - **Haunted:** You're often lost in reverie, reliving past horrors, seeing things.
  - **Obsessed:** You're enthralled by one thing: an activity, a person, an ideology.
  - **Paranoid:** You imagine danger everywhere; you can't trust others.
  - **Reckless:** You have little regard for your own safety or best interests.
  - **Soft:** You lose your edge; you become sentimental, passive, gentle.
  - **Unstable:** Your emotional state is volatile. You can instantly rage, or fall into despair, act impulsively, or freeze up.
  - **Vicious:** You seek out opportunities to hurt people, even for no good reason.
- Death: unresisted level 4 harm, or harm needed at level 3 when that row is full (catastrophic). On death the player may make a new scoundrel or transfer to the Ghost playbook.

## 4. Resistance and armour (pp. 32 to 33)

- Resistance roll: 1d per attribute rating; suffer 6 stress minus the highest die; critical also clears 1 stress. The consequence is reduced or avoided (GM chooses). Only one roll per consequence.
- Armour box instead of a roll: mark it to reduce or avoid; boxes restore at downtime.

## 5. Harm and recovery (p. 31, p. 155)

See `01-character-sheet-anatomy.md` section 4. Recovery: healer rolls Tinker (Physicker) or an NPC's quality; segments 1 to 3: one, 4/5: two, 6: three, critical: five. Self-treatment with Physicker costs 2 stress; toughing it out costs 1 stress and rolls 0d. New harm clears the healing clock.

## 7. Teamwork (pp. 134 to 135)

- **Assist:** take 1 stress, give +1d; only one assist per roll; you may share consequences.
- **Lead a group action:** everyone involved rolls the same action; the best result counts for all; the leader takes 1 stress for each PC whose best result was 1 to 3. Cohorts can be led (roll Command to direct, or your own action if you take part; the cohort rolls its quality).
- **Protect:** face a consequence in a teammate's place; you may resist it as normal.
- **Set up:** if your action succeeds, followers get +1 effect or improved position.

## 8. Plans and engagement (p. 127)

Six plans, each with a required detail:

| Plan | Detail |
|---|---|
| Assault: do violence to a target | The point of attack |
| Deception: lure, trick, or manipulate | The method of deception |
| Stealth: trespass unseen | The point of infiltration |
| Occult: engage a supernatural power | The arcane method |
| Social: negotiate, bargain, or persuade | The social connection |
| Transport: carry cargo or people through danger | The route and means |

Then each player chooses their load. Several claims and abilities give +1d to the engagement roll for a named plan type.

## 9. Gather information (pp. 36 to 37)

Effect level sets the detail: limited (incomplete), standard (good details, follow-ups possible), great (exceptional, complete). Example questions from the book's list, suitable for the sheet footer:

- What's really going on here?
- What are they really feeling? What do they intend to do?
- How can I find [X]? Where did the package end up?
- How can I discover leverage to manipulate them?
- What's a good point of infiltration? What's the danger here?
- Are they about to attack us?
- How can I get them to trust me?
- Have any new ghosts been here? What should I be worried about?

## 10. Coin, payoff and stash (pp. 42 to 43, p. 146)

- Coin values: 1 a month's wages; 2 a fine weapon or luxury clothes; 4 an exquisite jewel; 6 a small safe of valuables; 8 liquidating a carriage or a deed; 10 a treasure trove.
- Payoff by score: 2 minor, 4 small, 6 standard, 8 big, 10+ major. Tithe to a boss: coin equal to Tier +1.
- Coin can be recorded on the crew sheet or divvied among PCs.
- Stash conversion: 2 stash to 1 coin. Lifestyle level = full rows of ten.

## 11. Heat, wanted level and incarceration (pp. 147 to 149)

- Heat per score and modifiers: see `04-crew-sheet-anatomy.md` section 4.
- Sentence by wanted level: 4 life or execution; 3 a year or two; 2 several months; 1 a month or two; 0 a few weeks or a beating (level 3 harm, no resistance).
- Incarceration roll, 1d per crew Tier: critical +3 rep, a prison claim, +1 status with a faction helped inside; 6 a prison claim and +1 status; 4/5 uneventful; 1 to 3 a level of trauma.
- Entanglements (pp. 150 to 152) are rolled by the GM after each score based on heat and wanted level; not sheet content.

## 12. Downtime activities (pp. 153 to 159)

Two free activities per PC per downtime (one when at war); each extra costs 1 coin or 1 rep. After any downtime roll, spend coin to raise the result one level per coin.

| Activity | Roll | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Acquire asset | Crew Tier | Quality: 1 to 3 Tier -1; 4/5 Tier; 6 Tier +1; critical Tier +2. +1d if acquired before. |
| Long-term project | An action | Clock ticks: 1 to 3 one; 4/5 two; 6 three; critical five. Clocks are usually 4, 6 or 8 segments. |
| Recover | Healer's Tinker or NPC quality | Healing clock ticks as above. |
| Reduce heat | An action | Clear heat: 1 to 3 one; 4/5 two; 6 three; critical five. |
| Train | none | Mark 1 xp in an attribute or playbook track (2 with the crew Training upgrade); once per track per downtime. |
| Indulge vice | Lowest attribute | Clear stress equal to the highest die. |

**Vice roll details (pp. 156 to 157):** clearing more stress than you had marked is overindulgence, choose one: Attract Trouble (an extra entanglement), Brag (+2 heat), Lost (vanish for weeks; play another character; harm heals), Tapped (purveyor cuts you off, find a new one). Not indulging costs stress equal to your trauma count. Spider's Functioning Vice adjusts the die by 1 or 2; Cult's Conviction adds the Worship vice.

## 13. Advancement (pp. 48 to 49)

- PC: fill the playbook track (8) for a special ability; fill an attribute track (6) for an action dot in that attribute.
- Crew: fill crew XP for a special ability or two upgrade boxes; profits +1 stash per PC plus 2 per Tier.
- Changing playbook or crew type: rebuild with the same advances, or transfer ratings and upgrades (keep some abilities as Veteran advances).

## 14. Faction game (pp. 44 to 47)

- Tier, hold, rep and turf: `04-crew-sheet-anatomy.md` sections 2 and 3.
- Status levels: +3 Allies (help even against their interest), +2 Friendly (help unless it causes serious problems), +1 Helpful (help if no cost), 0 Neutral, -1 Interfering, -2 Hostile, -3 War.
- Status changes after operations: -1 or -2 with factions hurt, +1 with a faction helped; nothing if kept quiet. Seizing a claim: usually -2 with the target.

## 15. Crafting and alchemicals (pp. 224 to 227)

Relevant to the Leech's bandolier and gadgets, and to the Whisper's and Leech's known formulas:

- Crafting roll: 1d per Tinker dot; quality 1 to 3 Tier -1; 4/5 Tier; 6 Tier +1; critical Tier +2; +1 per coin spent. Without a Workshop, effective Tier is one lower.
- Inventing a new formula or design is an 8-segment long-term project (Alchemist, Artificer and Strange Methods each start with one known).
- Drawbacks: Complex, Conspicuous, Consumable, Rare, Unreliable, Volatile.
- Common alchemicals available to a bandolier (quality tier / uses): Grenade (III/2), Large Bomb (V/1), Smoke Bomb (II/2); Black Lotus (I/1), Bloodneedle (I/3), Dream Smoke (I/3), Quicksilver (IV/1), Spark (I/3), Trance Powder (I/3); Alcahest (III/1), Binding Oil (III/1), Fire Oil (III/3); Drown Powder (III/2), Eyeblind (III/3), Skullfire (III/1), Standstill (IV/1); Rage Essence (III/1), Shadow Essence (III/1), Viper Potion (IV/1). The Leech's sheet lists: Alcahest, Binding Oil, Drift Oil, Drown Powder, Eyeblind Poison, Fire Oil, Grenade, Quicksilver, Skullfire Poison, Smoke Bomb, Spark (drug), Standstill Poison, Trance Powder (p. 71).

## 17. Heritage, background and vice descriptions (pp. 53 to 55)

**Heritage:** Akoros (largest, most industrialised land, home of the capital and Duskwall). The Dagger Isles (corsairs and merchants who grow up on ships, live without lightning barriers). Iruvia (rich desert kingdom to the south, considered foreign). Severos (nomadic horse-tribes in the deathlands, considered wild). Skovlan (island kingdom, last brought under imperial rule in the 36-year Unity War; many refugees). Tycheros (semi-mythical, beyond the northern void sea; said to be part-demon; add a demonic telltale).

**Background:** Academic (scholar, professor, student, philosopher, journalist). Labor (servant, factory worker, coach driver, docker, sailor). Law (advocate, Bluecoat, inspector, Spirit Warden, Ironhook guard, Rail Jack). Trade (shopkeeper, merchant, crafts-person, shipping agent). Military (Imperial or Skovlander soldier, mercenary, intelligence operative). Noble (dilettante, courtier, scion of a fallen house). Underworld (street urchin, gang member, young thug, outcast).

**Vice:** Faith (an unseen power, forgotten god, ancestor). Gambling (games of chance, betting). Luxury (expensive or ostentatious displays). Obligation (a family, cause, organization, charity). Pleasure (lovers, food, drink, drugs, art, theater). Stupor (oblivion in drugs, drink, the fighting pits). Weird (strange essences, rogue spirits, bizarre rituals). Vice purveyors by type are listed on p. 299 and are already extracted in `Downloads/blades-in-the-dark-data/vice_purveyors.json`.
